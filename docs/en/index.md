## Onboarding a new customer shouldn't become a last-minute scramble.

Requirements arrive in documents, emails, spreadsheets, checklists, and portals. Every customer asks for different information, uses different terms, and structures the process differently.

Before your company can even start closing the gaps, someone has to understand what is actually being asked.

**Which documents are mandatory? What is only a recommendation? Does a requirement apply only in certain situations? What do we already have? What has expired? What still needs to be provided?**

This work is usually manual and scattered across people, files, and systems — and often the missing pieces only become clear when the deadline is already close.

**The result is a process that depends on manual interpretation, knowledge spread across the company, and constant follow-up — exactly when your company needs to respond quickly and with confidence.**

---

## From customer requirements to a clear plan of action.

### 1. Bring your customer's requirements

Start with what you received: a document, a list of requirements, or text describing the conditions for onboarding and qualification.

### 2. Understand what is actually being asked

Supplier Ready organizes the requirements, identifies what is mandatory or conditional, and flags what needs clarification — while keeping every conclusion connected to the original source.

### 3. Compare them with your company's reality

Tell Supplier Ready what your company already has and track which requirements are met, which need attention, and which still depend on action.

### 4. Know what's missing before you're ready

Supplier Ready turns all of this into a clear view of how prepared your company is — its **readiness** — showing what is already resolved and what still needs to happen before moving forward with the new customer.

## Understand not only the answer, but where it came from.

Supplier Ready uses AI to help interpret your customer's requirements without turning assumptions into facts.

**Traceable to the source**  
See the original passage that supports each identified requirement.

**Clear when there is uncertainty**  
Conditions and ambiguities stay visible until they can be clarified.

**No invented requirements**  
An AI inference does not silently become an obligation for your company.

> **When Supplier Ready doesn't know, it shouldn't guess. It should ask.**

AI helps interpret. The source remains the truth.

## Your customer may speak another language. Supplier Ready speaks yours.

Supplier Ready starts in Portuguese, English, and Spanish. Requirements can arrive in one language while you follow the analysis in another.

**The source stays original.**  
Every piece of evidence remains in the language in which it was received, preserving exactly what your customer said.

**The explanation comes in your language.**  
Requirements, conditions, and clarifications can be presented in the language you choose.

**The language changes. Traceability doesn't.**
